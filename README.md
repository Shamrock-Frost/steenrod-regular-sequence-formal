# steenrod-regular-sequence-formal

A Lean 4 and Mathlib proof that the Steenrod powers of the coefficients of the characteristic
polynomial of the generic matrix over a finite field form a regular sequence. It is the main
theorem of a paper of mine in preparation (Brendan Murphy, *Steenrod powers of the
characteristic polynomial form a regular sequence*).

## The theorem

Let `p` be a prime, `r ≥ 1`, `q = p^r`, and `k` the field with `q` elements. Let `R = k[y_ij]`
be the polynomial ring on the entries of the generic `n × n` matrix `Y`, and let `c_1, …, c_n`
be the coefficients of its characteristic polynomial,

    det(T·1 - Y) = T^n - c_1 T^(n-1) + ⋯ + (-1)^n c_n.

The total `q`-Steenrod power is the `k`-algebra map `R → R[T]` that sends each variable `x` to
`x + x^q T`, and `P^i(f)` is the coefficient of `T^i` in the image of `f`.

**Theorem.** For every `n`, the `n(n+1)/2` elements `P^i(c_j)`, `0 ≤ i < j ≤ n`, form a regular
sequence in `R`.

Since `P^0(c_j) = c_j`, `P^j(c_j) = c_j^q` and `P^i(c_j) = 0` for `i > j`, these are all the
Steenrod powers of the `c_j` other than zero and the powers `c_j^q`.

In Lean ([`Height/Chain/Boxes.lean`](Height/Chain/Boxes.lean)):

```lean
theorem Height.Chain.regular (p r n : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] [NeZero r] :
    Height.Steenrod.SteenrodRegularSequenceStatement p r n
```

where ([`Height/Steenrod/MainStatement.lean`](Height/Steenrod/MainStatement.lean))

```lean
def Height.Steenrod.SteenrodRegularSequenceStatement
    (p r n : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime] [NeZero r] : Prop :=
  RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular (QSteenrod.GenericMatrixRing p r n)
    (QSteenrod.powerCharacteristicCoefficients p r n)
```

Names are written out in full here; the source opens the namespaces. The list
`powerCharacteristicCoefficients` is ordered by increasing `j` and, for each `j`, by increasing
`i`.

The hypothesis `[NeZero r]` says `r ≥ 1`. The Lean definitions also make sense at `r = 0`, where
the exponent `q` is 1, and there the statement fails for `n ≥ 2`, because `P^1(c_2) = 2 c_2`.

## The proof

Let `J` be the ideal generated by the `P^i(c_j)`. In a polynomial ring in `n^2` variables over a
field, `ht J + dim R/J = n^2`, and `ht J ≤ n(n+1)/2` because `J` has that many generators. So
the bound `dim R/J ≤ n(n-1)/2` gives `ht J = n(n+1)/2`. The generators are homogeneous of
positive degree and `R` is Cohen–Macaulay, so they are then a regular sequence.

Most of the work is the bound on `dim R/J`. In outline:

1. A matrix `M` over an extension field of `k` is a zero of `J` exactly when `sM + tM^(q)` is
   nilpotent for all `s` and `t`, where `M^(q)` is `M` with every entry raised to the `q`-th
   power (`powerCharacteristicCoefficients_pencil_criterion`).
2. In particular `M` is nilpotent, so the zero locus is covered by its intersections with the
   finitely many nilpotent conjugacy classes over the algebraic closure. Inside the class of a
   nilpotent `N` defined over `k`, Lang's theorem shows that the zero locus has the same
   dimension as the set of partners of `N`: the `M` in the class for which every `sN + tM` is
   nilpotent (`Height.Chain.untwist`).
3. The partners for which `N` and `M` become strictly upper triangular in a common basis are
   bounded through the dimension of the Springer fibre of `N` (`Height.Chain.springer`).
4. The remaining partners are bounded by two estimates on incidence varieties of pencils
   (`Height.Chain.pencilIncidenceBound` and `Height.Chain.markedIncidenceBound`). Each rests on
   an isotropy statement for tangent directions: one for a residue pairing attached to the
   pencil `sN + tM` over the projective line (`Height.Pencil.residue_eq_zero`), the other for
   the Kostant–Kirillov form on the nilpotent cone (`Height.Chain.nilcone_kkIsotropic`).

[`Height/Chain/Edges.lean`](Height/Chain/Edges.lean) has the top of this chain in order, with
the paper's labels next to the names of the theorems.

## Checking it

The statement depends on the definitions in [`QSteenrod/`](QSteenrod), two files of about 160
lines: [`Basic.lean`](QSteenrod/Basic.lean) defines the operations `P^i`, and
[`Characteristic.lean`](QSteenrod/Characteristic.lean) the coefficients `c_j` and the ordered
list of the `P^i(c_j)`. The rest is Mathlib: `MvPolynomial (Fin n × Fin n) (GaloisField p r)`
for `R`, `Matrix.charpoly.univ` for the characteristic polynomial, and
`RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular`. The fork described below changes none of the files these are
defined in.

There is no `sorry` in the source and no axiom is declared. To see the axioms the theorem
depends on, after building:

```sh
echo 'import Height
#print axioms Height.Chain.regular' | lake env lean --stdin
```

This prints `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.

## Dependencies

Mathlib is pinned to commit
[`76e9e72`](https://github.com/Shamrock-Frost/mathlib4/commit/76e9e72564e982c2424e0d218a34f96bfc712bbc)
of a fork: Mathlib master of 3 September 2026 plus nine pull requests by other contributors
that were open at the time, on depth and Cohen–Macaulay rings, on Krull dimension under
integral extensions, and on Noether normalization. The fork's
[merge commits](https://github.com/Shamrock-Frost/mathlib4/commits/76e9e72564e982c2424e0d218a34f96bfc712bbc)
name them. This project uses them for the formula `ht J + dim R/J = n^2`, for the step from
height to regularity, and in the dimension theory of affine varieties.

## Layout

- [`QSteenrod/`](QSteenrod): the definitions of `P^i` and `c_j`.
- [`Height/Steenrod/`](Height/Steenrod): the ideal `J`, its zero locus in terms of nilpotent
  pencils, Lang's theorem, and the statement.
- [`Height/Chain/`](Height/Chain): the proof of the dimension bound and of the theorem, 27
  files and 16,000 lines. Start at [`Edges.lean`](Height/Chain/Edges.lean).
- [`Height/Geometry/`](Height/Geometry): affine varieties in coordinates, 27 files and 13,000
  lines. Dimension and fibre dimension, tangent spaces and arcs, affine group schemes and their
  actions, the formula `ht J + dim R/J = n^2`, and the Cohen–Macaulay step.
- [`Height/Pencil/`](Height/Pencil), [`Height/Graded/`](Height/Graded),
  [`Height/DVR/`](Height/DVR): pencils of matrices, graded modules over `K[μ, ν]`, and modules
  over `K⟦u⟧`.
- [`Height/Orbital/`](Height/Orbital): strata of nilpotent matrices, arcs in them, and the
  Kostant–Kirillov form.
- [`Height/Toolbox/`](Height/Toolbox): lemmas in linear and commutative algebra used by the
  rest.
- [`Isotropy/`](Isotropy): trace and commutator lemmas over a principal ideal domain and over a
  field, used by `Height/Pencil` and `Height/Chain`.

Labels in the doc-comments such as `thm:main` and `lem:residue-form` are those of the paper.

## Building

Install [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan), then:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

The cache covers the files of upstream Mathlib. The files the fork adds or changes, and the
Mathlib files that import them, are compiled locally. Once Mathlib is in place, the 165 files of
this project take about four minutes on six cores.
